Accounts without a password

You can create, fund, use and recover a devicebasedauth.org account with only a device and a card. No password, and no sign-in provider: signing in with Clerk (Google, GitHub or email) is optional.

What this means, and how we know

Your first deposit creates the account, and the device that paid becomes its first way in: its key never leaves it, and the account's id (acct_…) is not tied to any email address or third-party login. Everything an account can do, an account made this way can do.

This is tested on every change. src/clerk-free-accounts.test.js runs one account's whole life (sign up, use, deposit again, confirm with a passkey, create an API key and a second device, lose every device, recover) on a server with no Clerk configured, and fails if any step so much as reads a Clerk setting or uses a route only a Clerk session can reach. The command-line sample below runs in CI too.

Create an account

  • In a browser: Create an account. Choose a first deposit, name the browser, accept the terms, and pay on Stripe Checkout. You come back signed in.
  • From a terminal: dba signup --accept-terms prints the Checkout link, waits, and saves the credential (see below).
  • From your own page: the browser SDK (v1.3.0+) has signUp({ creditCents, acceptTerms }), then finish() after Checkout.

The first deposit is $5 to $20 (new accounts may deposit at most $20 per payment in their first week); the card fee is itemized on top, and the credit never expires. Nothing is stored until the payment arrives: Stripe's signed notice creates the account. The device's credential (dba:session, the dashboard) costs the usual issue price from the new balance.

For ten minutes after paying, the new device counts as recently signed in, so you can set up a way back in straight away: recovery codes, a passkey, another device, or an email address.

Confirming it is you

Some operations hand someone else control of your account if a device is stolen while unlocked, so they need a confirmation from the last ten minutes. A device asks one of your gates: a passkey or security key, an authenticator app, or another of your devices typing the two-digit number shown. On the dashboard that is the Confirm it is you button; in the API, POST /api/v1/session/reverify. An account with no such gate cannot do these operations until it adds one, which the ten minutes after paying are for.

Getting back in

If you lose every signed-in device, recover your account onto a new one. Recovering is free (an empty balance never locks you out), and for ten minutes afterwards you can revoke the lost devices and make new codes. Every recovery is announced to your browsers and by email.

Way back inHowTrade-off
Recovery codesTen one-time codes and your account id, on a sheet you keep.Immediate. Whoever finds the sheet can get in, so keep it away from your devices.

An email address verified hereWe email a link; once it is used, the new device gets in after 72 hours, unless any signed-in device cancels it.The weakest: whoever controls the mailbox can start it, so the wait gives you time to notice and cancel.
NoneYou accept, on the Account page, that losing every device loses the account.Nothing to steal, and nothing to fall back on: the account and its balance are gone with your last device.

Authenticator-app codes confirm it is you but do not recover an account: the app usually lives on the phone that was lost. The Account page shows which ways you have and warns while there are none.

Adding a password sign-in

An account made by paying can add a Clerk sign-in as another way in (Account page, Add a password sign-in; POST /api/v1/account/clerk-link), and unlink it later if you keep another way in. A Clerk account can likewise stop using Clerk: daily use already works from its devices, and deleting the Clerk user never closes the account.

From the command line

# 1. Sign up: a new key, a link to pay the first deposit by card (a local server takes no
#    card), then a dashboard credential and a recovery sheet. Keep the sheet somewhere safe.
dba signup --accept-terms --deposit 5 --name "Sample laptop" | tee "$DBA_HOME/sheet.txt"

# 3. The laptop is lost. On another machine (here another profile), a recovery code puts a new
#    key on the account; for ten minutes that counts as a recent sign-in.
dba --profile spare recover --account "$account" --code "$code" --name "Sample spare"

From docs/samples/signup-and-recover.sh, which runs in CI against a local server. dba recover --account acct_… --via gate asks your passkey or another device; dba recover --email you@example.com starts an email recovery; either finishes later with --resume.
